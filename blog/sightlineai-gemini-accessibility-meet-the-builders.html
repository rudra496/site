SightlineAI: Helping Blind People Navigate the World with Google Gemini

By Rudra Sarker • Published September 18, 2026 • Meet the Builders — Gen AI Academy APAC

Open Source · MIT License · Research Archived

SightlineAI — AI-Powered Accessibility for the Visually Impaired

🌐 Live demo  ·  💻 Source on GitHub  ·  📄 Zenodo DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22118700

The problem I couldn't ignore

I grew up in Sylhet, Bangladesh. Here — like across much of South Asia — visual impairment is not a rare condition. The WHO estimates that more than 285 million people worldwide live with severe visual impairment, and a disproportionate share live in low-income regions where a guide dog, a $3,000 smart cane, or a commercial accessibility wearable is simply out of reach.

The gap is not intelligence or willingness — it is access. The smartphone in a blind person's pocket already has a camera, a microphone, and a speaker. The only missing piece was the intelligence layer that turns those sensors into a pair of eyes. That thought became SightlineAI: an open-source, AI-powered accessibility companion that transforms an ordinary device into real-time environmental awareness for the visually impaired.

What SightlineAI does

Point a camera at the world, and SightlineAI speaks the parts that matter:

  • Real-time scene understanding — what's around you, described in natural language
  • OCR & document reading — signs, labels, menus and printed text read aloud
  • Obstacle & hazard awareness — risk-weighted alerts before you reach the hazard
  • Context-aware navigation support — guidance layered on OpenStreetMap data
  • Conversational assistance — ask follow-up questions about your surroundings
  • Multi-language support & offline fallback — because connectivity in a developing region is a "when it works", not an "always"

Everything streams over WebSockets so guidance arrives in near real time — not after a loading spinner.

The build story

I'm a final-year Industrial & Production Engineering student at Shahjalal University of Science and Technology (SUST). I didn't come to this problem from a medical-device background — I came to it as a builder who kept meeting blind students and street vendors navigating my city without any assistive technology at all.

The hard architectural lesson was that you cannot run heavy multimodal models on the devices people actually own. So SightlineAI decouples the system: the edge device handles what it's good at — camera capture, voice input, speech playback — while heavy cognition runs in the cloud, bridged by a FastAPI backend with WebSocket streaming, Redis for session state, and Docker for deployment. React powers the companion interface.

That "end-cloud collaborative" split is what makes the system viable on low-end hardware — and it is also what makes it genuinely responsive.

Powered by Google AI

The intelligence layer is where Google Gemini earns its place:

Capability What Gemini does in SightlineAI
Multimodal reasoningFuses camera frames + user context into a coherent scene description
Scene understandingIdentifies objects, obstacles, signage and spatial relations
Accessibility guidanceConverts raw perception into safe, actionable voice guidance
Conversational Q&ALets the user ask "what's in front of me?" and get grounded answers

Gemini's multimodal nature was decisive. A classical CV pipeline needs a separate model per task — detection, OCR, captioning, dialogue — each with its own latency and failure modes. With Gemini, a single multimodal reasoning call covers the full chain from pixels to spoken guidance, which cut pipeline complexity dramatically and kept response times practical for real-time use. The system is provider-agnostic by design (Qwen is supported as an alternative), but Gemini is the default brain — and the Google AI stack is what the primary experience is built and tested on.

Impact & what's next

SightlineAI maps to UN SDG 3 (Good Health & Well-Being), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities) and SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities & Communities). The next milestones: Bangla-first voice UX, low-cost phone-only deployment kits for local blind schools, and community localization of the guidance layer.
